# Frequently Asked Questions

## What is first-party data?
First-party data is information that companies collect from their own sources. This can be collected from both online and offline sources such as their website, app, social media, or surveys. At Immediate, we collect first-party data on our users using our DMP, Permutive. This includes information about the content they're looking at, the time of day or week they're looking at it, the device they are browsing from, and their location.

## Where will the campaign be delivered?
Campaign delivery depends on the setup in Google Ad Manager. While users in our segments may be seen across multiple IM sites, they will only be served impressions on the sites specified in the campaign setup.

## What's the difference between Permutive and Audience Project?
Permutive segments are built based on content users actively browse. We can segment users based on how often and when they have been looking at specific content. Audience Project, on the other hand, is a platform we use to deploy surveys onto our sites. Using survey responses, we build targetable segments with a panel of 400k users in the UK. Audience Project allows us to create segments for which we do not have relevant content on our sites, such as online gaming habits.

## How does the logic behind Audience Project work?
The segment data we have are probabilistic derived profiles based 100% on behavioural data algorithms, delivering a very high degree of precision. The precision on the algorithms is constantly validated against Audience Project's massive online panel of 400k active panelists in the UK.

## How do you target audiences without cookies?
Unlike some other publishers, Immediate Media uses a cookie-less data collection and targeting method known as local storage. Although there are similarities between local storage and 1st party cookies, the main difference is how data is accessed. Cookie data is sent back to the server on every web request, whereas local storage is not. Local storage only makes sense at a per-domain level, meaning it cannot be used to stitch together a profile of a user across multiple sites like 3rd party cookies. You can think of local storage as storing information which can only be read client-side (on your computer). Cookies relate to information which needs to be read server-side.

## How do you define "fans," "enthusiasts," and "intenders"?
- **Fans** — Views related content at least 2 or more times
- **Enthusiasts** — Views related content at least 6 or more times
- **Intenders** — Views at least 4 or more product/car reviews

## What data points do we collect using Permutive?
General data points include: article title, URL, tags, type (recipe or how-to article), geographic information (city, country, postal code), technographics (device, internet provider), recipe info (title, ingredients, cooking time, cuisine, course, diet type, nutrition info, keywords, skill level), product info (category, name, price, target age), and car info (body style, doors, make, model, fuel type, insurance category, price).
